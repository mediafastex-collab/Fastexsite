# Projection Room

**A design philosophy for moving images held in stillness.**

Projection Room begins in darkness. A field of deep, warm black — never pure, always carrying a faint ember of orange at its centre, the way a cinema still glows after the lights go down. Space is not empty here; it is anticipation. Every composition is built around a single illuminated frame, and everything else exists to make that frame feel lit from within. The work must look meticulously crafted: the darkness graded by hand, the light falling exactly where the eye should land, nothing accidental.

Form follows the logic of a projector's beam. One frame stands upright and true at the centre, full scale, sharp-edged. Its neighbours recede into depth — turned a few degrees away, smaller, cooler, quieter — so the eye reads a sequence without being told one exists. Rounded rectangles in a strict 9:16 proportion are the only containers. Their corners, gaps and angles are tuned with painstaking attention until the arrangement feels inevitable, as if the frames found their own resting places.

Colour is rationed like light in a dark room. Charcoal and ink for the ground; bone-white for the few words that matter; a single brand-orange reserved for one thing only — the measure of time passing. Orange appears as thin, exact lines: a progress rule that fills, a numeral that is currently speaking. Used anywhere else it would be noise. The restraint itself is the craft, the product of deep expertise in knowing what to leave out.

Rhythm comes from numbering and duration rather than decoration. Large, thin serif numerals — 01, 02, 03, 04 — stand like reel labels in the margin, with titles set small beside them. Time is drawn as a ruled timeline: fine ticks at regular intervals, one segment glowing as it advances. Typography is whispered: a refined serif for names, a precise sans or mono for labels, letterspaced and quiet. Text is never explanation; it is signage in a dark theatre, there only to orient.

Composition balances weight against void. The lit frame sits slightly off the true centre, counterweighted by the column of numerals and a generous expanse of shadow. Margins are wide and exact; nothing touches an edge, nothing collides. The finished piece should read as master-level execution — the kind of composition that took countless refinements to look this calm — inviting the viewer to stay, to watch, and to press play.
