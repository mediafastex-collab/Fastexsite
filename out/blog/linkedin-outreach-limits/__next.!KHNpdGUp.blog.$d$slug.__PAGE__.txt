9:T11ed,<p>Every few months someone comes to us with the same problem. They scaled LinkedIn outreach, it worked for a fortnight, and then the account got restricted. Now the profile they spent years building is in read only mode and nobody knows why.</p>

<p>It's almost always the same cause, and it's almost always avoidable.</p>

<h2>The numbers we actually run</h2>

<p>LinkedIn does not publish a hard limit, which is why the advice online is all over the place. What exists is a weekly invitation cap that tightens or loosens based on how your account behaves.</p>

<p>Here is what we run across managed profiles, and these are deliberately conservative.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Connection requests:</strong> 20 to 25 a day on a warmed, established profile.</li>
<li><strong>New or recently quiet profiles:</strong> start at 5 to 10 a day and climb over three weeks.</li>
<li><strong>Profile views:</strong> keep them proportionate. Hundreds of views with no other activity looks automated.</li>
<li><strong>Messages to existing connections:</strong> higher ceiling, but still spread through the day.</li>
</ul>

<p>Could you push to 40 a day? For a while, yes. The question is whether two extra weeks of volume is worth the account.</p>

<h2>What actually triggers a restriction</h2>

<p>Volume alone is rarely the whole story. It's volume combined with behaviour that doesn't look like a person.</p>

<p><strong>A low acceptance rate.</strong> This is the big one. If fewer than about 30% of your invitations get accepted, LinkedIn reads it as spraying. Tighter targeting fixes this faster than lower volume does.</p>

<p><strong>Mechanical timing.</strong> Exactly 25 requests, exactly 9am, exactly every weekday. Real people are irregular. Spread activity through working hours and vary the daily number.</p>

<p><strong>Logging in from two countries at once.</strong> A tool running on a server in one country while you use the app in another is an obvious flag. Keep sessions on a consistent location.</p>

<p><strong>Being reported.</strong> Enough people marking your message as spam will restrict you at any volume. This is a copy problem, not a limits problem.</p>

<h2>If you are already restricted</h2>

<p>Stop all automation immediately. Everything. If a tool is still running while you appeal, the appeal fails.</p>

<p>Then use the account normally for a week or two. Read your feed, comment on things, reply to messages. Appeal through the official form, and be straightforward about it. Restrictions on a first offence are usually temporary. Restrictions on a second are often not.</p>

<p>The recovery takes longer than the restriction did to earn, which is the whole argument for staying under the ceiling in the first place.</p>

<h2>The approach that doesn't need limits</h2>

<p>Here is the thing worth sitting with. Teams that obsess over limits are usually compensating for a list that isn't good enough.</p>

<p>If your acceptance rate is 60% because you're contacting people with a genuine reason to connect, 20 requests a day produces more conversations than 40 requests at a 20% acceptance rate. You get more pipeline, and you keep the account.</p>

<p>Better targeting isn't the cautious option. It's the higher output one.</p>

<p>We run outreach for clients at 20 to 25 requests a day per profile, and add profiles when more volume is needed rather than pushing a single account harder. It is slower to set up and it does not end with a message explaining why the founder's profile is locked.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>How many LinkedIn connection requests can I send per day?</h3>
<p>Around 20 to 25 a day on an established profile is the safe working range, and a new or recently inactive profile should start at 5 to 10 and build over about three weeks. LinkedIn applies a weekly invitation cap that tightens if your acceptance rate is poor, so the ceiling is partly set by your own targeting.</p>

<p>Automation itself is against LinkedIn's terms, and heavy, mechanical activity is what gets detected. Most restrictions come from a low acceptance rate, perfectly regular timing, or sessions running from two locations at once rather than from the volume alone.</p>
